The 12th fret repeats the open string one octave up — learn frets 0–12 and you know the whole neck. Naturals (no sharps/flats) are brighter; those are the ones to lock in first.

An interval is just the distance between two notes — and on guitar it couldn't be simpler: 1 fret = 1 semitone (a “half step”), and 2 frets = a “whole step”. Every interval name is really just a number of frets from the root. Tap one to see and hear it.

📋All the intervals at a glance

The orange dot is the root; the blue dot is the interval. The “frets up” number is literally how far to move. So when a quiz says “a perfect 4th”, it just means 5 frets up — which is also why the next string over at the same fret is a 4th higher.

🔢What the numbers mean

Chord names look like math, but every number is just a step of the major scale. Start on the root and count up — and past 8, the numbers keep climbing instead of resetting. Tap one to hear its color (the root plus that note).

Chords are built by stacking every other note, so we skip by odd numbers: 1 → 3 → 5 → 7 → 9 → 11 → 13. The big ones are just the small ones an octave higher — the 9 is the 2, the 11 is the 4, the 13 is the 6. And a ♭ or ♯ in front (like ♭7 or ♯11) simply nudges that one note a fret down or up.

🔑Chords that belong together

These seven chords are all built from C major, so any of them sound good together — they're one family. Each has a job:

● Home — feels resolved  ·  ● Away — feels like motion  ·  ● Tension — pulls back home

A progression is just a trip through those jobs — most go Home → Away → Tension → Home. Safe bets that always work in the key: I–IV–V, ii–V–I, I–vi–IV–V, vi–IV–I–V.
